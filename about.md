I’m a data engineer who enjoys understanding how systems work and turning complex problems into reliable solutions.

Over time, I learned that building a pipeline is only one part of the work. The real challenge is understanding why the data is needed, how it moves across the system, where it can fail, and what will keep the solution reliable in production.

## How I got here

I studied mechanical engineering at Kalinga Institute of Industrial Technology. That background taught me to look at a system as connected parts rather than isolated components. When something goes wrong, I want to understand the interaction between those parts and trace the problem to its cause.

I carried that way of thinking into data engineering. At Accenture, I worked with retail and inventory data through Oracle RMS ETL, PL/SQL, Python, FastAPI and production batch support. At NBC Bearings, I worked with manufacturing data using Azure Databricks, PySpark, SQL, FastAPI and Delta Lake. The work included ingestion, transformation, validation, monitoring, troubleshooting and deployment checks.

These experiences gave me two useful views of a data system: how it is designed and how it behaves after it starts running.

## How I approach the work

I begin with the reason the data is needed and the decision it should support. Then I look at the sources, keys, movement, transformations and failure cases. I try to keep the overall architecture in view while working through the details.

Reliability also means being able to explain an output. Missing records, duplicate events, schema changes, failed joins and late data should be visible. Monitoring and recovery are part of the design, not work to add after a pipeline is finished.

My portfolio projects follow the same approach. The manufacturing project connects machine, sensor and maintenance records. The incident project explores how evidence can be collected for an investigation. The supply-chain project starts with source contracts and consistent business keys.

## Where AI fits

As AI becomes part of more businesses, I see strong data foundations as essential. Analytics, machine learning, generative AI and Physical AI all depend on data that can be understood, checked and traced.

I’m interested in AI that retrieves relevant information, explains the available evidence and helps a person investigate a problem. For consequential decisions, the person responsible for the work should remain in control.

## What I’m looking for

I’m continuing to build my skills in data platforms, Spark processing, streaming, lakehouse systems, observability and recovery. I’m interested in opportunities where I can take ownership, learn a new domain, solve meaningful problems and build solutions that create real business value.
